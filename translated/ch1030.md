# Chapter 1030: Entering the Dao Overnight, Luo Chen Awakens

The Eastern Sea had changed; sea demons had appeared.

The Central Continent, which had been peaceful for many years, could no longer suppress those restless elements after its fragmentation.

Over a decade ago, shadows of sea beasts had occasionally appeared near the Tiannan Continent. Recently, however, they had become more frequent and increasingly impatient.

During the Zhou clan's latest sea-patrol mission, they encountered a sudden, large-scale attack by a swarm of sea demons.

At that time, there were few high-level cultivators stationed there. Under the surprise assault, every clan inevitably suffered heavy casualties.

The Zhou clan was no exception.

A total of seven Initiate cultivators and over thirty Qi Refinement cultivators had died in battle.

For the Zhou clan, which was in the midst of recovery, this was undeniably a heavy blow.

The atmosphere in the Zhou clan had been quite somber in recent days. Due to the funerals, the sounds of weeping and wailing lingered long into the quiet nights.

Inside the council hall, a voice filled with anger suddenly exploded.

"Damn it! Who told you to inform him of his parents' death so bluntly three days ago?"

The current clan head, Zhou Junxiao, glared at the woman before him, his face full of fury.

Zhou Shuzhen was puzzled. "Second Brother, what's the problem? Zhou Fuyou has always been known for his precocious intelligence, and he's the only pillar left for Baihan's family. It was perfectly normal for me to tell him the news, wasn't it?"

"The procedure wasn't wrong, but..." Zhou Junxiao shook his head, still dissatisfied. "He's still just a child!"

Zhou Shuzhen's red lips parted slightly as she stood there stunned.

In the eyes of outsiders, Zhou Fuyou's advanced development made it hard to associate him with children his own age. He was almost indistinguishable from a normal teenager. Coupled with his mature speech, it was easy to forget his actual age.

When she had been notifying the close relatives of the fallen clan members one by one, she had indeed overlooked this.

Zhou Junxiao earnestly instructed her, "Be more careful in the future. Now that the Ancestor is not handling affairs, the First Brother is in seclusion, focused on breaking through to the Sage realm, and the other two have only recently formed their cores. The clan's affairs rely on you and me to manage. When you encounter problems, discuss them with me more."

"Alright, Second Brother. I'll remember that," Zhou Shuzhen said, then watched as Zhou Junxiao turned to leave. "Second Brother, where are you going?"

Zhou Junxiao paused, recalling the order the Ancestor had sent him, and sighed softly.

"I'm going to see that child."

He left the council chamber and headed towards the place where the vigil was being held.

Soon, he stood outside the mourning hall.

Inside, many people were either standing or kneeling—all relatives of the clan members who had died in this battle. Most were young, but there were also those who had to bury their own children. The sight of white-haired individuals sending off the black-haired was not uncommon.

And that thin, delicate-looking boy was kneeling before a coffin, silently burning paper money.

His expression was calm. Though there was sorrow on his face, there were no tears.

Zhou Junxiao observed quietly for a while, then nodded to himself.

"He's a resilient one."

...

The burial took place four days later. The entire funeral ceremony lasted seven days.

On the day of the interment, all Zhou clan members set aside their affairs and, dressed in white mourning clothes, participated in the final ritual.

Zhou Fuyou stood before the tombstone, his lips tightly pressed together, holding the hand of his sister, whose face was streaked with tears.

Occasionally, sympathetic and pitying glances were cast his way, but he seemed oblivious.

"Brother, are Father and Mother really gone?"

His sister still seemed unable to accept reality.

Zhou Fuyou had no choice but to pick her up and comfort her in a low voice.

Seeing the girl still looking lost and only knowing how to cry, Zhou Fuyou said in a deep voice, "Father and Mother are gone, but I'm still here. Big Brother will protect you from now on."

"Will Brother leave too?"

Zhou Fuyou's gaze fell on the tombstone as he gently stroked his sister's head.

"Big Brother will never leave!"

The boy's words were soft, but they were as firm as a mountain, as decisive as a blade.

As the clan head, Zhou Junxiao, activated the cemetery's protective formation, the funeral officially came to an end.

Although the sounds of weeping and remembrance still echoed, the clan members eventually returned to their homes to continue their lives.

That night.

After coaxing his sister to sleep, Zhou Fuyou once again entered his father's study.

The scene was still so familiar, yet he could no longer see that tall man, nor hear that gentle voice at his ear.

As the warm memories of the past replayed in his mind, the boy who had seemed so resolute in front of others found tears silently streaming down his face.

Suddenly!

He thought of the bowl of medicinal soup he had drunk, of Yin Siniang's mysterious death, and of the calm indifference on Elder Zhou Shuzhen's face when she announced his parents' deaths.

A chill surged into his heart.

In a great aristocratic clan, while there was warmth, there was always more of the power struggles and deceit found in stories.

Those without value did not deserve attention.

His parents, who could have been the pillars of heaven and earth for him and his sister, were gone. To survive, to ensure his sister grew up well, he could rely on no one but himself!

Zhou Fuyou wiped away his tears, walked to the bookshelf, and deftly took out several books.

Holding a thick stack of books, the boy was about to return to his desk, but after a moment's hesitation, he pulled out another book from the corner.

Spreading all the books out, familiar names came into view. These were the Qi Refinement scriptures he had read over the past six months.

"I want to cultivate!"

"I need power!"

"No one can be trusted. I must protect my sister!"

His eyes swept over each book, his mind constantly weighing and considering. Finally, his jet-black, ink-dot-like pupils settled on the *Dew Cicada Art*.

The other techniques were all too slow and had various thresholds and restrictions.

Only this demonic technique had the fastest cultivation speed and no restrictions whatsoever. As long as one possessed a spirit root, they could cultivate it.

So, did Zhou Fuyou have a spirit root?

He believed he did!

Without any artifact or formation to test it, and at an age too young, the boy made the most reckless judgment.

But this judgment had a certain basis.

It was what the world called "spiritual qi affinity"!

Without a spirit root, one could not sense the existence of spiritual qi. With a spirit root, one could feel the spiritual qi present in heaven and earth. The type of spirit root one had determined which corresponding attribute of spiritual qi they could sense. The easier and deeper this sensation, the higher the spiritual qi affinity, and the better the spirit root attribute.

Legend had it that those with a Heavenly Spirit Root could even avoid the interference of other chaotic spiritual qi, specifically sensing the spiritual qi of their corresponding attribute, resulting in extremely fast cultivation speeds.

In truth, spirit roots existed within the human body from birth. The reason for waiting until the age of twelve was that, at that age, a child's body was relatively mature, their mind was sound, and they could sense the existence of spiritual qi more clearly.

Even so, this sensitivity was still very low.

That was why cultivators needed to set up corresponding formations, provide a large and balanced amount of spiritual qi, and use artifacts to test for specific spirit roots.

But Zhou Fuyou was different!

From a very, very early age, when he was very, very young, even as far back as his earliest memories, he could sense an omnipresent energy surrounding him.

At first, he didn't know what that thing was.

But once, during a meal, his mother said that the food contained faint spiritual qi, which helped nourish their bodies. From then on, he knew what the energy around him was.

Spiritual qi!

He had been able to sense spiritual qi since he was a child, and there was a high probability it was water-attributed spiritual qi, because the sensation was even stronger when it rained.

This meant he had the highest affinity for water-attributed spiritual qi!

In other words, he should possess a water-attributed spirit root.

With this, the constraints on practicing techniques no longer existed.

Zhou Fuyou couldn't wait until he was twelve. He felt danger was everywhere, that no one was trustworthy, that everyone wanted to harm him—except his sister.

"Then I'll start cultivating now!"

Having made up his mind, the boy spread out the *Dew Cicada Art* and, following the posture described in the book, sat cross-legged on the cold stone floor of the study.

He formed a hand seal in a passable imitation and began to breathe in a rhythmic pattern.

According to the records in the scriptures, the difficulty of the first cultivation session did not appear.

With just the first breath, the energy surrounding him eagerly surged into his body.

The boy was caught off guard, yet it seemed he had expected it. He guided that energy towards his dantian, following the meridian path of the *Dew Cicada Art*.

Throughout this process, the energy never ceased. Whenever it seemed to wane, more energy from the outside world would flood into his body.

It was as if, as long as he wished it, as long as his body could bear it, this heaven and earth would bestow upon him an inexhaustible supply of energy.

Outside the window, a white light flashed, followed by the sound of thunder.

*Rustle, rustle!*

In the height of summer, a torrential downpour began.

Through the half-open window, the boy remained motionless.

It wasn't until his body felt a sensation of fullness that he slowly opened his eyes, gently exhaled a breath of turbid qi, and looked at the world with a somewhat dazed expression.

The sky had already brightened.

The faint light of dawn streamed in, falling upon Zhou Fuyou, casting a shimmering glow.

With a thought from Zhou Fuyou, that peculiar fluctuation of spiritual power completely dissipated, as if nothing had ever happened.

But only the boy knew that he was now a cultivator.

"Entering the Dao overnight, Qi Refinement, First Layer!"

...

The Silver River stretched far and wide, the starry river vast and boundless.

In that utter silence, a black-robed Daoist sat cross-legged in the void, motionless, as if devoid of any breath.

Only the sharp, dragon-like streams of air occasionally expelled from his nostrils indicated that he was still alive.

Not far from him, a large grey cauldron stood beneath the stars.

The cauldron vibrated incessantly, and from within came all sorts of beastly sounds. There were angry roars, endless wails, and amidst them, chaotic voices of cursing, begging for mercy, and threatening.

But whether it was the silence of the cosmos, the vastness of the stars, or the beastly roars that seemed to sound right in his ear, none of it could disturb the black-robed Daoist.

Suddenly!

The black-robed Daoist's eyelids trembled slightly, and he slowly opened his eyes.

His lips moved slightly, uttering three words.

"Qi Refinement stage."

Then, the black-robed Daoist frowned, performed a quick calculation with his fingers, and a look of confusion appeared on his face.

"Six years old?"

"Why did he start cultivating at six years old?"

A wisp of green smoke rose from the cauldron beside him, coalescing into a graceful, enchanting woman.

She spoke in a crisp voice, asking curiously, "Master, what's happened?"

The black-robed Daoist shook his head. "It's nothing major, but... it's too fast!"

Murmuring to himself, the black-robed Daoist fell into thought.

After a long while.

He made a decision.

"Xiao Bai, go to the Zhou clan in the Tiannan Continent and take a look at one of my avatars for me."

The woman called Xiao Bai nodded, then asked, "Should I provide protection?"

The black-robed Daoist thought for a moment before saying, "Unless absolutely necessary, do not interfere in anything related to him. Your main task is surveillance. Take this sound transmission jade. If anything unexpected happens, you can notify me at any time."

Xiao Bai took the jade. Before leaving, she cast a worried glance at the grey cauldron.

"Master, the refinement of that Kui Ox Ghost God has reached its most critical stage, and its resistance is growing fiercer. You must be careful not to let it escape."

The black-robed Daoist smiled faintly.

"It's fine. Since I was able to subdue it, I'm not afraid of it breaking free."

Xiao Bai smiled, then drifted away.

Luo Chen was not worried about Xiao Bai getting into trouble.

After refining a considerable number of soul stones, Xiao Bai had already reached the mid-fourth-order stage.

As long as no Grand Cultivator made a move, given her characteristics as a ghost cultivator, very few could threaten her.

And on the Tiannan Continent, there were only two Grand Cultivators, both residing in the Tiannan Dao Palace.

Going to the Zhou clan was a simple matter. As long as she was careful, perhaps even Zhou Yunshen wouldn't be able to detect her.

He was more worried about his own avatar. Starting to cultivate at just six years old was incredibly reckless.

Didn't he have any elders to teach him to proceed step by step?

Although he had told Zhou Yunshen not to interfere too much with the avatar's growth, shouldn't the most basic things be guided by his parents or elders?

"Perhaps something unexpected has happened."

Luo Chen muttered to himself, then resumed refining the Red Firmament Sword Qi.

Before going into seclusion, he glanced at the Primordial Unity Cauldron, his gaze icy cold.

...

Half a year later.

On a square in the White Egret Marsh, over thirty youths stood in neat rows, listening to the teachings of an old man with white hair.

"You have all passed the age of six and must begin preparing for the spirit root test and cultivation six years from now."

"Next, I will teach you a set of body-tempering techniques to strengthen your physiques."

"Do not underestimate body refinement. If you are found to have a spirit root and can cultivate in the future, a strong physique will provide no small help in the early stages of cultivation, at least allowing you to bear the pain of spiritual qi entering your body. Even if you don't have a spirit root, you can use this to embark on the martial path, protecting yourself in the mortal world. It counts as a useful skill."

The youths all shouted in unison, their voices loud and clear.

The old man was very satisfied with their response. Then, on the raised platform, he began to perform a set of boxing techniques in a gentle, flowing manner.

This boxing technique was not particularly powerful, nor did it have much killing potential, but it was very beneficial for exercising the body, working many different areas.

The youths all followed along, imitating his movements as best they could.

After they had all memorized the forms, the old man stopped demonstrating and sat to the side, letting them practice on their own.

When someone encountered a problem and came to ask, he would patiently answer, without any airs.

The old man was well aware of his position. He was not an exalted cultivator, but merely a mortal Xiantian martial artist who had been assigned this task by the clan.

If he did his job well, he could not only obtain longevity-extending pills for himself but also build many connections for his descendants.

After all, among the youths on this square, there were bound to be some with spirit roots, who would one day become lofty cultivators.

Zhou Fuyou was among them.

Not only him, but his sister Zhou Qingyu was also there, practicing this body-strengthening boxing technique.

However, unlike Zhou Fuyou, who picked it up very quickly, Zhou Qingyu was very slow and even seemed to lack stamina. Soon, sweat was beading on her face.

"Maomao, if you're tired, go rest."

"Brother, I'm not tired."

The little girl's face showed excitement, as if she was very interested in cultivation.

But, being frail and prone to illness, she tripped and fell to the ground in a moment of carelessness.

Zhou Fuyou quickly helped her up, gently blowing on the red mark on her elbow.

This heartwarming scene, however, drew snickers from the side.

"A sickly child should just stay home and recuperate. Why come here to join the fun? Don't tell me you think you can become a cultivator someday!"

A pair of eyes focused on them.

Zhou Qingyu's big eyes instantly welled up with tears.

Zhou Fuyou raised his head, his cold eyes landing on the boy not far away, and he uttered words that were chilling to the bone.

"Apologize."
