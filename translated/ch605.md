# Chapter 605: Great Zhou Royalty — Encounter with a Dragon at the Lake Bottom

"Oh, I met him, all right."

Recalling the brief half-day meeting just concluded, and the bearing, demeanor, realm, and cultivation that the man had displayed — even the well-traveled Moya Cave Master could not help a quiet measure of admiration.

"Even as a wandering cultivator with no sect at his back, Devil Lord Azure Yang's bearing is no less than that of a true regional lord. Compared to you, Fellow Daoist Huangfu, he holds his own without yielding."

The words were spoken sincerely.

But reaching Huangfu Song's ears, they grated.

"My Huangfu bloodline descends from the royal clan of the most powerful dynasty the Northern Sea ever produced. A mere wandering cultivator — however well-regarded — cannot be spoken of in the same breath as me."

"Oh?"

The Moya Cave Master looked at him with a faint, unreadable smile.

"If that is so, why trouble me on his account at all — why repeatedly press his supposed capabilities on me?"

"Or was Fellow Daoist Huangfu trying to provoke me deliberately? Hmm?"

The final drawn-out syllable stretched the smile into something faintly unsettling.

Huangfu Song's expression shifted slightly.

This man — known throughout the Assembly's wandering cultivators for his artifact-forging mastery and not generally known for sharp-tongued word games — was now trading barbs with him as if every word had been weighed in advance.

What had passed between him and that Devil Lord Azure Yang in the course of half a day at Peng Lake?

What Huangfu Song didn't know was that someone who had clawed their way up through the wandering cultivator world by mastering a single craft — as the Moya Cave Master had — was not someone lacking in verbal skill. He simply, having reached a certain level, had grown too indifferent to bother with petty quarrels. Today, however, after turning things over during his return journey, he had gradually realized he had likely been used as a weapon.

A blade wielded by someone else's hand.

Under the Cave Master's steady gaze, Huangfu Song forced a recovery. "I wouldn't stoop to such scheming. Fellow Daoist has truly misunderstood me."

"Heh."

Seeing the Cave Master's light laugh, Huangfu Song pressed on with his reframing.

"It's simply that I've heard this person is skilled at manipulating public perception — first there were suspicions about inflating the reputation behind his title, and then in just a few years he contrived the fame of 'Artifact-Forging Master Azure Yang.' If he truly establishes himself, the title of the Assembly's foremost artifact-forger will be taken from you."

"Of course, a mere name would be beneath your notice, Cave Master. But for us who cultivate the Way, a name is a golden mark — it touches vast interests and can influence our very dao practice."

"For this reason, I have repeatedly urged you: do not let this person grow too large."

The more he spoke, the smoother the argument flowed.

By the end, even Huangfu Song had nearly convinced himself.

The Moya Cave Master set aside his smile and studied the purple-robed man before him seriously.

The man's face was openly composed — without much visible artifice.

Yet the Cave Master, having lived as many years as he had, knew that controlling one's facial expression was a simple matter for a cultivator of this level in all but the most severe circumstances. He had no intention of taking Huangfu Song's words at face value.

After a long silence.

"Let this matter rest here."

With that, he turned and stepped toward the depth of the cloud mist.

Huangfu Song was not willing to let it go, and hurried after him.

"Cave Master — are you truly not concerned about Devil Lord Azure Yang growing stronger and threatening your instrument trade in the Assembly? You have several hundred cultivators who depend on you, Moya Cave Master. Even if you yourself don't care, those people —"

The Cave Master raised his right hand and cut him off.

"You worry too much. I had a brief exchange with Azure Yang on matters of the forging craft. His foundations are solid, but his approach is scattered — he has yet to find his own style. Without an established style, can one truly be called a master? And without that title, how would he threaten my standing in the Assembly?"

"But what if he was being deliberately modest?"

"Heh. Words can deceive. But the work a craftsman produces cannot deceive my eyes."

The Moya Cave Master radiated quiet confidence.

"The most celebrated instrument to Azure Yang's name is nothing more than an upper-grade Nine Dragon True Fire Canopy. After careful analysis, I found that this piece is actually a variant of the Nine-Turning Flame Dragon Grand Formation — a top-tier second-tier offensive formation. He reversed the approach and repurposed a destructive formation array as a defensive instrument."

"Clever, certainly. But it loses the essence of what a formation is."

"With his current level of mastery in the forging craft, he poses no threat to me for at least a hundred years."

"Fellow Daoist Huangfu, you genuinely worry too much."

Huangfu Song opened his mouth — and found for once that he could not counter.

Or rather, knowing nothing of the forging craft, he had no idea how to respond.

It was the Cave Master who stopped walking, turned back, and looked at him.

"I hear that in recent years, you have been working steadily to gather the former subjects of the old Great Zhou dynasty, seeking to restore the glory of the royal house. It seems you've assembled quite a number of talented people under you — and that you now urgently need a large spirit land to serve as your base of cultivation."

Huangfu Song's heart gave a lurch; the corners of his mouth tightened involuntarily.

The Cave Master caught that flicker of expression.

He shook his head and sighed.

"The great dynasty model is not suited to those of us who walk the cultivation path. The Great Zhou existed in its time because the Primordial Demon Sect supported it. But that was also its undoing — the Sect that built it could destroy it, and so could any other power. As Old Moya sees it, Fellow Daoist Huangfu would be better served focusing on his own cultivation rather than these entanglements."

"With your natural gifts, the Great Zhou's legacy, and the backing of Fellow Daoist Purple Dowager — you may have better odds at the Nascent Soul path than most of us."

He clasped his hands.

"I've said enough. For the near future, Fellow Daoist need not call on me at Moya Cave. I've taken on an order from an old friend and have no time to spare."

With that, he drifted at an easy pace into the cloud mist.

This time, Huangfu Song did not follow.

Standing where he was, his expression shifted through several shades.

At last, he shot a look of restrained resentment toward the direction the Cave Master had gone.

"That old fellow — ordinarily unremarkable, never seeming to calculate anything — yet dealing with people with such thoroughness. He clearly saw through me and still went out of his way to give me genuine advice."

He cursed under his breath — but did not actually hold the Moya Cave Master in any ill will.

The words the man had spoken were honestly meant.

But —

How could Huangfu Song abandon a great enterprise left half-done?

Especially now, when the world was in upheaval and the Northern Sea in turmoil — the perfect moment to fish in troubled waters and build one's own power.

This was not ambition for its own sake.

In truth, a lone cultivator had a poor chance at breaking through to Nascent Soul.

The Purple Dowager?

That senior had been stuck at Golden Core Stage Nine for years without moving. Even the path of rising through the demon hunter ranks to earn the Assembly's support had become empty fantasy — the nine-star rank stubbornly refused to come. She could barely tend to herself, let alone offer him more.

Only by planting one's own power, gathering talent, and building one's own force — and then drawing on the Great Zhou's legacy — was there even a sliver of a chance at forming a Nascent Infant.

Thinking about it, Huangfu Song's mood turned sour.

The words were easy to say. But the very first step was where things had stalled.

Building a power, gathering talent — at the very least, one needed a stable base where those people could settle their hearts and cultivate.

Eight-Hundred-Li Peng Lake was precisely that ideal piece of spirit land — more than enough to carry him through the early stages of this undertaking.

But he had not expected: one trip away, and upon return, Peng Lake was already occupied.

An ordinary wandering cultivator, and he might have managed it. But the occupant was Devil Lord Azure Yang — ruthless, bold, and utterly fearless.

A war of words would never convince a person like that to yield Peng Lake.

His original plan had been to cut off the man's finances through an indirect hand — use the Moya Cave Master to suppress the instrument trade that served as his steady income stream. When the five-year lease expired and the man's reserves ran dry, he would step in and take over Eight-Hundred-Li Peng Lake without lifting a finger.

Evidently, that scheme had fallen apart.

Huangfu Song had no desire to wait any longer.

*If the indirect approach fails — try the direct one.*

*That Devil Lord Azure Yang is alone with no one to back him. If I come to his door in person, he'll have no choice but to yield.*

At the thought of what was to come, the irritation in Huangfu Song's heart eased somewhat, and a look of composure returned to his face.

...

Inside the Star-Dipper Hall on Peng Island.

Heavenjade stepped in quickly, caught for a brief moment by the hall's splendor — before her familiarity with it pulled her back.

"Master, this is the intelligence report you requested. I retrieved it from the Demon Hunter Office — the Angler delivered it personally."

"Did he have anything to convey?"

Luo Chen accepted the thick bundle of bamboo slips and set them on the writing table.

Heavenjade nodded. "He did ask me to pass something on. The Angler has run into a problem in his body cultivation — he'd like to arrange a time to discuss it with you."

This did not surprise Luo Chen.

The path of body tempering looked simple from the outside — merely a matter of resource accumulation. In truth, every checkpoint had its own thresholds.

He himself had spent his whole cultivation career feeling his way forward, with guidance from a good teacher like Wang Yuan and the occasional benefit of the system — and had only barely reached this point.

The Angler thinking he could advance easily was wishful thinking.

If the man truly succeeded effortlessly, it would negate a hundred years of Luo Chen's hard cultivation.

"The next time you go to Dragon Abyss Immortal City, let the Angler know: I have the time. He can set the meeting place."

Heavenjade acknowledged it, and as she was about to leave, she suddenly remembered something.

"Master — the five-year lease on Peng Lake is coming up. The Angler asked whether we're renewing."

"Renew. Of course."

Luo Chen smiled. As for the nine hundred thousand spirit stones required, he was not particularly pressed for funds.

Of the original eighteen million, he had spent half — but the remaining half was still there. True, that remaining half was only enough for another five years, but Luo Chen was not anxious.

Not because his instrument trade brought in especially large profits. That business, across a full year, netted perhaps ten or twenty thousand in pocket money — enough to break even on the costs of forging, no more.

His real reserve lay in another stockpile entirely.

The spoils from Cold Light Island — diverse resources, miscellaneous law devices he had no use for, and various obscure items — all still sat unprocessed in his possession.

By his estimate, liquidating all of it would bring in one or two million more spirit stones.

After all, it represented the lifetime wealth of several Golden Core cultivators.

Including Qian Ting, the head of a Golden Core family clan.

Spending that down would sustain the Peng Lake lease for another twenty or thirty years.

And twenty or thirty years from now, whether through improved instrument trade or a return to alchemy, there would be plenty to sustain him.

In short, spirit stones were not a near-term concern.

After Heavenjade left.

Luo Chen unrolled the first bamboo slip.

At the top: *History of Northern Sea Dynasty Succession — The Great Zhou.*

Below that:

*Above stood the Soul Division, the holy land hung high in the heavens. The Northern Sea had its master, and its name was the Primordial Demon Sect. Its cultivators, though of an overbearing nature, still maintained the cultivator's spirit of devoted practice. The resources they required were paid in annual tribute by the Nascent Soul upper sects beneath them. Beyond these, the Sect also commanded secular dynasties directly answerable to it, managing the production of basic resources. Qian, Zhuang, Zhou, Yong — all were subordinate powers of their time. These dynasties were mostly Golden Core forces, administered by direct disciples of the Primordial Demon Sect, effectively serving as proving grounds. The Great Zhou, however, was an anomaly — for within the Great Zhou dynasty, a Nascent Soul true being had quietly emerged...*

Reading the plain, direct account on the bamboo slip, Luo Chen's brow gradually creased.

A sect controlling secular nations — this model was not unfamiliar to him.

In the Jade Cauldron Domain, Qingdan Valley had overseen a nation called the Yuanzhao Kingdom.

To his knowledge, other sects controlled several dynasties of varying sizes as well.

These secular nations served mainly to sustain populations and supply candidates with spirit roots for the immortal cultivation path — and to gather basic resources needed in large quantities.

But the Great Zhou described in this slip had clearly exceeded the definition of an ordinary secular dynasty.

*Their ancestors actually produced a Nascent Soul true being?*

...

"Move faster, move faster!"

"The family head said it clearly — this batch of fish fry must be released at designated points. The count per zone has to be exact."

"Gentle, gentle! These are Golden Dragon Carp — grown adults are first-tier demon beasts. Raise them well and they might advance to second tier. If you damage even one fry, be ready to compensate at least five hundred spirit stones."

"Don't argue with me that the fry aren't worth five hundred — I'm billing you for a fully grown first-tier Golden Dragon Carp!"

On the mist-hazed surface of the lake, small boats spread outward from Cailian Island in every direction.

At every ten-li interval, a batch of fry was released at a designated spot.

And simultaneously, large quantities of feed were scattered into the water below.

The feed had been ground from spirit-qi-infused medicinal herbs — one of the Golden Dragon Carp fry's preferred foods. Long-term feeding would accelerate their growth and help them reach adult size ahead of schedule.

This feed was not cheap.

For this grand aquaculture venture, Zhang Jiadi had scraped together every last resource he had — and still could only fund one-tenth of what was needed. At a critical moment, it had taken fifty thousand spirit stones from Immortal Heavenjade on Peng Island to get the first batch of Golden Dragon Carp to a viable scale.

With that kind of investment at stake, not one thing could be taken lightly.

For this, the entire Zhang clan on Cailian Island had mobilized.

Everyone — from Foundation Establishment cultivators down to mortal martial artists — turned out in full, with only Wen Xiu remaining at Azure Yang Hall in Dragon Abyss Immortal City to keep things running.

Zhang Jiadi sat in a small boat, carefully releasing a batch of fry himself.

Straightening up, he wore a smile full of hope.

Golden Dragon Carp had exquisitely tender flesh and were suffused with spiritual qi — one of the most beloved delicacies among Northern Sea cultivators.

The only reason they weren't already as prized as spirit rice was that their habitat requirements were strict — they could only be farmed in places with ample spiritual qi.

Originally, the idea hadn't been on his mind at all.

It was only through a chance conversation with a Azure Yang Hall customer, learning the details of what had happened on Cold Light Island, that the thought had occurred to him.

Apparently that battle had taken place in the Golden Dragon Carp fish farm belonging to a Golden Core family clan. The destruction had been so complete that every carp in the farm had perished — with far-reaching aftereffects. Golden Dragon Carp had become scarce, and the market price had skyrocketed several times over.

After learning those details, he had formed this idea.

Eight-Hundred-Li Peng Lake: excellent water quality, abundant spiritual qi, an ocean of aquatic vegetation at the lake bottom — extremely well-suited for Golden Dragon Carp farming.

No one had tried it before — first because no one knew the husbandry techniques, second because the startup costs were prohibitively high, and third because wandering cultivators were accustomed to rootless, wandering lives and rarely invested in fixed operations, which was why the eight hundred li had sat empty all these years.

But for a cultivation family committed to multi-generational continuity, like the Zhangs, none of these obstacles applied.

Most importantly — his wife Wen Xiu knew how to raise Golden Dragon Carp.

After consulting Immortal Heavenjade, the great aquaculture venture had launched in full.

"Even though the main location and resources came from our master, the Zhang family is contributing labor and personnel — and we'll earn a tenth of the proceeds."

"Once the operation grows to scale, that tenth could bring in tens of thousands a year."

Zhang Jiadi smiled, as if already imagining the scene of casting out great nets and hauling in countless leaping, gleaming adult Golden Dragon Carp.

Then — suddenly — he looked left and right.

"Where's Qingqing?"

A nearby low-level cultivator answered: "The young miss took the puppet white horse you gave her and went out to play. She didn't board the boat earlier."

"She ran off again?" Zhang Jiadi's brow creased. He would have thought twice before giving that gift.

The white horse was a Tier-Two puppet with wave-treading and sky-crossing capabilities. Operation was simple enough that even a mortal could manage it with the right switch.

Their daughter Qingqing was not yet five, but her mind was quick and her disposition lively and spirited.

Since receiving the white horse, she had been away from home nearly every day.

"Family head, shall I go find the young miss and bring her back?"

"Let her run wild."

Zhang Jiadi shook his head. She was playing within Eight-Hundred-Li Peng Lake, and Immortal Heavenjade had given her a protective talisman. As long as she didn't go charging onto Peng Island itself and interrupt the master's cultivation, there would be no real trouble.

When this work was done, he'd go find her himself.

Such, it seemed, was what it meant to be a father.

...

On the tranquil surface of the lake, green lotus leaves spread in endless layers, touching the sky in every direction.

Some pink buds, still closed, awaited the moment of bloom.

Then — a breeze swept through.

And with it, a figure burst out from among the lotus leaves.

"Hyah! Hyah! Hyah!"

A childish voice called out in excitement, too high to tell boy from girl at first hearing.

A rosy-cheeked little girl rode the white horse, skimming the waves at speed.

Clear of the great lotus field, she flung out an arm behind her.

*Snap!*

A paper kite caught the wind and shot into the sky.

The girl's eyes lit with excitement. One hand gripping the horse, the other clutching the kite's handle — she felt the speed was not nearly fast enough.

She twisted the horse's throttle.

"Faster, horse, faster!"

"If Mama finds out, she won't let me play like this anymore."

As the throttle turned, the white horse surged — faster and faster. The girl began to feel the strain.

For all that she had been nourished by spirit medicines from birth and raised in a place where spiritual qi soaked into her day by day, lending her a strength and health unusual for her age — she was, in the end, a child not yet five years old.

Unchecked, the kite suddenly climbed high — and the handle was wrenched from her grip, falling into the water.

The girl startled. On instinct she twisted around to grab for it.

*Splash.*

She went into the water.

Beneath the surface she kicked and struggled — water surging at her from every direction.

But the moment it touched her skin, a cyan light shield shimmered and pushed it back.

"Hm?"

The little girl paused. She reached into her robes and pulled out something small.

A feather — a gift from Auntie Heavenjade.

From the feather, ring after ring of cyan light spread outward, gently parting the water.

"This is fun!"

Now even more excited, the girl forgot entirely about the white horse.

Her great round eyes spun with delight; gripping the feather, she dove straight down toward the lake bottom.

"Mama always tells me not to go in the water. Hmph — today I'm going to see what's down there."

Feather in hand, the little girl moved through the water like a small fish, diving steadily deeper.

The deeper she went, the greater the water pressure grew — but beneath the cyan light, she felt none of it.

Without noticing, the girl began to feel the first signs of effort.

Just as she was thinking of turning back — her body froze rigid.

"What is that?"

Deep in the lake bottom, two orbs of light — like a pair of lanterns — suddenly blazed to life.

*(End of Chapter)*
